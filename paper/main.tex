\begin{abstract}
% RESUMO (escrever por último; máx. ~250 palavras). Estrutura obrigatória, uma ou duas frases
% cada: (1) contexto: homogeneização biótica apaga fronteiras e é difícil de medir;
% (2) lacuna: classificadores usados sem validação rigorosa e sem teste direto da hipótese;
% (3) o que fizemos: dados + ML validado (baselines, CV repetida, permutação, AUC por pares);
% (4) três números-chave: acurácia balanceada vs. nulo; AUC Pan x Cer vs. Pan x Fpl; espécies;
% (5) ressalva de desenho (1 área por paisagem; geografia e campanha);
% (6) implicação para conservação/monitoramento.
% NÃO usar "prova", "comprova", "corrobora integralmente".
Biotic homogenisation erases the compositional boundaries between ecosystems, but it is difficult to
measure from small biodiversity surveys. We asked whether social-wasp assemblages (Vespidae:
Polistinae) identify the landscape they come from, and whether an anthropised wetland in the Pantanal
has become less distinguishable from a dry savanna (Cerrado) than from a preserved floodplain, as
homogenisation predicts. Using \res{n-records} independent records of \res{n-species} species at
\res{n-points} points in three protected areas of Mato Grosso do Sul, Brazil, we compared seven
classifiers under repeated cross-validation, permutation tests and class-imbalance corrections, and
tested homogenisation directly through the separability (AUC) of each pair of landscapes. The best
model reached a balanced accuracy of \res{best-balacc}, far above the null expectation. The floodplain
was almost perfectly separable from the Pantanal (AUC = \res{brf-auc-panfpl}), whereas the Pantanal
and the Cerrado could barely be told apart (AUC = \res{brf-auc-pancer}; difference
\res{brf-dauc}). The convergence rested on a few widespread swarm-founding species, chiefly
\taxon{Agelaia bequaerti}, and held across \res{sens-n-scenarios} sensitivity scenarios. Because each
landscape is a single area, and the Pantanal and Cerrado sites are geographically closer and were
sampled in the same year, the pattern is compatible with, but not proof of, anthropogenic
homogenisation. We argue that validated, imbalance-aware classifiers turn ``the model confused two
habitats'' into a quantitative, testable measure of lost ecological identity.

\paragraph{Ecological identity and its loss.}
% P1: definir identidade (combinação característica de espécies) e homogeneização (aumento da
% similaridade entre locais por perda de especialistas e expansão de generalistas). Citar
% McKinney & Lockwood 1999; Olden & Rooney 2006; exemplos recentes em insetos (Ogan 2022,
% Fisogni 2025) e um exemplo de alto impacto (Aulus-Giacosa 2024, Nature Communications).
Every ecosystem harbours a characteristic combination of species, so that the list of species
recorded at a site carries information about the environment it came from. Human disturbance can
erode this identity: specialists disappear, a few tolerant generalists spread, and assemblages of
formerly distinct places become more similar to each other, a process known as biotic homogenisation
\citep{mckinney1999,olden2006}. Homogenisation reduces beta diversity, the component of biodiversity
that conservation planning relies on to prioritise distinct places \citep{socolar2016}, and it has
now been documented in insect groups as different as grasshoppers \citep{ogan2022}, bees
\citep{fisogni2025} and ants at a global scale \citep{aulus2024}, against a background of
widespread insect decline \citep{wagner2021}.

\paragraph{Why it is hard to measure, and what machine learning offers.}
% P2: matrizes de comunidade (muitos zeros, poucas espécies comuns, relações não lineares).
% Testes clássicos (PERMANOVA) dizem SE os grupos diferem, mas não QUANTO um grupo se tornou
% indistinguível de outro, nem em que direção. Classificadores respondem a outra pergunta:
% "dado só a lista de espécies, consigo dizer de onde veio a amostra?" -> a separabilidade é a
% própria medida de identidade. Citar Cutler 2007, Pichler & Hartig 2023, Tuia 2022,
% e um parágrafo curto sobre ML em biologia de alto impacto (AlphaFold, Wang 2023).
Detecting homogenisation is an analytical challenge. Community data are sparse, dominated by a few
common and many rare species, and their responses to the environment are rarely linear
\citep{cutler2007,legendre2012}. Classical multivariate tests such as PERMANOVA \citep{anderson2001}
tell whether the composition of groups differs, but not how far one group has become
indistinguishable from another, nor in which direction. Supervised classifiers answer a
complementary question: given only the species recorded at a site, can its landscape of origin be
recovered? If it can, the landscape has an identity; if two landscapes are systematically confused,
their identities overlap. Tree-based ensembles such as random forests \citep{breiman2001} are well
suited to this question because they handle zero-inflated predictors and interactions without
distributional assumptions \citep{cutler2007,evans2011}, and they remain the workhorse of machine
learning in ecology even as deep learning transforms image- and sequence-based biology
\citep{pichler2023,tuia2022,wang2023,jumper2021,hoye2021}.

\paragraph{Machine learning as a measuring instrument requires validation.}
% P3: AQUI está a contribuição metodológica. Com n pequeno, a acurácia de um único modelo pode
% ser otimista ou pessimista por acaso; classes desbalanceadas empurram predições para a classe
% maior (o que imitaria "homogeneização"); pontos vizinhos inflam a acurácia (Ploton 2020;
% Roberts 2017). Checklists REFORMS (Science Advances) e DOME (Nature Methods).
% Frase de fechamento: "we treat the classifier as a measuring instrument and calibrate it".
A classifier is only a measuring instrument if its readings are calibrated. With small samples, a
single accuracy estimate can be inflated or deflated by chance; with unequal class sizes, errors are
pushed towards the larger classes, which can mimic the very confusion one wishes to interpret
\citep{chen2004,valavi2021}; and when nearby samples share information, random cross-validation can
greatly overstate predictive skill \citep{roberts2017,ploton2020}. Consensus guidelines for
machine-learning-based science therefore ask for explicit baselines, uncertainty and leakage control
\citep{kapoor2024,walsh2021}. Here we follow those guidelines and, instead of reading homogenisation
from the off-diagonal cells of a single confusion matrix, we test it directly as a difference in
separability between pairs of landscapes.

\paragraph{Study system.}
% P4: descrever o "laboratório natural". Áreas úmidas dependem do pulso de inundação (Junk
% 1989) e deveriam ter faunas mais parecidas entre si do que com áreas secas. Apresentar:
% PEVRI (várzea preservada, proteção integral), APA Baía Negra (Pantanal, uso sustentável,
% antropizada -- DESCREVER QUAL antropização: pecuária, supressão, urbanização de Ladário?),
% PN Serra da Bodoquena (planalto seco, Cerrado). Citar Leite et al. (companion) para detalhes.
% Já antecipar que as três áreas estão a distâncias diferentes (Pan-Cer mais próximas).
Floodplain wetlands are structured by the flood pulse \citep{junk1989}, which creates habitats
that differ sharply from the surrounding uplands; wetlands are therefore expected to share more of
their fauna with each other than with dry savannas. Mato Grosso do Sul, Brazil, offers three
protected areas that span this contrast: a preserved floodplain on the Ivinhema River (Várzeas do Rio
Ivinhema State Park), an anthropised wetland of the Pantanal (Baía Negra Environmental Protection
Area) and a dry plateau dominated by Cerrado vegetation (Serra da Bodoquena National Park)
\citep{leite_companion}. \TODO{describe the land use and disturbance history at Baía Negra in one or

\paragraph{Social wasps as biological sensors.}
% P5: por que vespas? (a) ninhos dependem de substrato vegetal, água, fibras e presas;
% (b) Epiponini fundam colônias por enxameagem e fazem ninhos grandes -> sensíveis à estrutura
% do habitat (Richards 1978; Jeanne 1991); (c) respondem à sazonalidade/microclima (Jacques
% 2023) e à conservação da vegetação (De Castro 2021; Francisco 2023; Leite 2026).
Social wasps (Vespidae: Polistinae) are promising sensors of habitat condition. Their colonies
depend on vegetation for nest substrate, on plant fibres and water for nest building, and on
arthropod prey, and the swarm-founding Epiponini build large, long-lived nests that require
structurally complex vegetation \citep{richards1978,jeanne1991}. Polistine assemblages respond to
vegetation type and conservation status \citep{decastro2021,francisco2023,leite2026} and to the
seasonal drought that shapes Neotropical dry environments \citep{jacques2023}.

\paragraph{Aims and hypotheses.}
% P6: FECHAMENTO. Escrever objetivos numerados (O1-O4) que correspondem 1:1 às subseções de
% Métodos e Resultados, e hipóteses com predições numéricas e falseáveis.
We asked two questions: (i) does the composition of the social-wasp assemblage identify the
landscape of origin above chance, and (ii) is the assemblage of the anthropised wetland less
distinguishable from the dry savanna than from the preserved wetland? We pursued four objectives:
(O1) to quantify how well wasp composition predicts the landscape, against explicit baselines and
with permutation tests; (O2) to test homogenisation directly through the separability of pairs of
landscapes; (O3) to identify the species that draw or blur the boundaries; and (O4) to evaluate the
robustness of these conclusions to class imbalance, spatial dependence, data encoding, model
settings and sampling campaign. We predicted that (H1) the preserved floodplain has a strong
identity, being separable from both other landscapes ($\AUC > 0.9$), and that (H2) the Pantanal
assemblage has converged on the Cerrado, so that $\AUC_{\Pan\times\Cer} < \AUC_{\Pan\times\Fpl}$
and Pantanal samples receive a higher predicted probability of Cerrado than of floodplain.

\subsection{Community composition}\label{sec:mcommunity}
% Conteúdo: Jaccard binário; PCoA; PERMANOVA global e par a par (Holm); PERMDISP (para
% separar diferença de centroide de diferença de dispersão). Apêndice A tem as fórmulas.
Compositional dissimilarity between points was measured with the Jaccard index on presence--absence
data and represented by principal coordinates analysis \citep[PCoA;][]{gower1966}. Differences among
landscapes were tested with PERMANOVA \citep{anderson2001} with \res{nperm-community} permutations,
followed by pairwise PERMANOVAs with Holm-adjusted $p$-values, and differences in multivariate
dispersion with PERMDISP \citep{anderson2006} (Appendix~\ref{app:community}).

\subsection{Classifiers (O1)}\label{sec:mclassifiers}
% Conteúdo: lista dos modelos com o PAPEL de cada um (nulo, baseline, principal, robustez).
% Hiperparâmetros FIXADOS a priori (sem tuning) -> justificar (Varma & Simon 2006: evita
% otimismo e dispensa CV aninhada; sensibilidade em O4). Mencionar a replicação do RF do
% rascunho (ranger, 1000 árvores, mtry 4, OOB = \res{oob-acc-draft-replication}).
We compared seven classifiers of increasing flexibility (Appendices~\ref{app:logistic}--\ref{app:boosting}):
(i) a null model that predicts the most frequent class of the training data, the reference for
``no biological information''; (ii) a multinomial logistic regression with $L_2$ penalty on
standardised frequencies, a linear and interpretable baseline; (iii) a $k$-nearest-neighbour
classifier ($k=5$) with Jaccard distance, the predictive counterpart of similarity-based community
analysis; (iv) an unweighted random forest, equivalent to the model of the first analysis of these
data (\citealp{wright2017}; out-of-bag accuracy \res{oob-acc-draft-replication}); (v) a random forest
with class weights inversely proportional to class frequency; (vi) a balanced random forest, in
which every tree is grown on a bootstrap sample with the same number of points per class
\citep{chen2004}; and (vii) gradient-boosted trees of depth two. Forests had \res{ntrees} trees and
$\lfloor\sqrt{p}\rfloor$ candidate species per split. All hyperparameters were fixed a priori at
standard values rather than tuned, which keeps the cross-validated estimates free of selection
optimism \citep{varma2006}; sensitivity to them was evaluated in O4. The balanced random forest was
designated in advance as the main model, because it removes the bias towards large classes that
could otherwise manufacture the confusion predicted by H2.

\subsection{Validation and inference (O1)}\label{sec:mvalidation}
% Conteúdo: CV estratificada 5-fold repetida 50x; mesmas partições para todos os modelos
% (comparações pareadas); métricas no conjunto agregado de predições fora da amostra de cada
% repetição; métricas: acurácia balanceada (principal), F1 macro, kappa, log-loss, recall por
% classe e taxa Pan->Cer; IC = percentis 2,5-97,5 sobre repetições; teste de permutação.
% Explicar POR QUE acurácia balanceada (classe Pantanal menor).
Performance was estimated by stratified five-fold cross-validation repeated \res{nrep} times. The
same partitions were used for every model, so that differences between models are paired. In each
repetition every point is predicted once by a model that did not see it, and metrics were computed
on these pooled out-of-fold predictions (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}). The primary metric was
balanced accuracy, the mean of the per-class recalls \citep{brodersen2010}, which, unlike overall
accuracy, is not inflated by the larger classes; we also report macro-averaged $F_1$, Cohen's
$\kappa$ \citep{cohen1960}, the multiclass log-loss, the recall of each landscape and the proportion
of Pantanal points predicted as Cerrado. Intervals are the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles over
repetitions. Whether a model performed better than chance was tested by permuting the landscape
labels and repeating the whole cross-validation \citep{ojala2010}, with \res{nperm-cheap}
permutations for the fast models and \res{nperm-forest} for the forests.

\subsection{Direct test of homogenisation (O2)}\label{sec:mhomog}
% Conteúdo: CENTRAL. Classificador binário por par de paisagens (BRF e logística), AUC fora da
% amostra (média de 20 repetições de CV), IC por bootstrap estratificado, diferença
% Delta = AUC(Pan x Fpl) - AUC(Pan x Cer) por bootstrap PAREADO (pontos do Pantanal
% reamostrados uma vez para os dois AUCs). Complementos: probabilidades das 3 classes para os
% pontos do Pantanal (Wilcoxon pareado) e o análogo sem modelo (distância de Jaccard média do
% ponto do Pantanal às outras áreas). Explicar a interpretação: AUC 0,5 = indistinguíveis.
To test H2 we fitted, for every pair of landscapes, a binary classifier using only the points of
that pair, and measured its out-of-fold separability with the area under the ROC curve,
\[
\AUC_{a\times b} = P\!\left(\hat s(\mathbf{x}_a) > \hat s(\mathbf{x}_b)\right),
\]
the probability that a randomly chosen point of landscape $a$ receives a higher score than one of
landscape $b$ \citep{hanley1982}. An AUC of 0.5 means that the two assemblages are
indistinguishable to the model and an AUC of 1 that they are perfectly separable. Scores were
averaged over \res{nrep-pairwise} repetitions of five-fold cross-validation, and uncertainty was
obtained with \res{nboot} stratified bootstrap resamples of the points \citep{efron1993}. H2 predicts
$\Delta = \AUC_{\Pan\times\Fpl} - \AUC_{\Pan\times\Cer} > 0$; because both AUCs involve the same
Pantanal points, $\Delta$ was bootstrapped in a paired way, resampling the Pantanal points once for
both terms (Appendix~\ref{app:auc}). The test was run with the balanced random forest and repeated
with the logistic regression. As complementary evidence we compared, for each Pantanal point, the
out-of-fold probabilities of Cerrado and floodplain given by the three-class model (one-sided
Wilcoxon signed-rank test), and the model-free mean Jaccard dissimilarity of each Pantanal point to
the Cerrado and to the floodplain points.

\subsection{Species that define the boundaries (O3)}\label{sec:mspecies}
% Conteúdo: importância por permutação calculada SÓ em pontos de teste (fora da amostra),
% p-valor por permutação dos rótulos (Altmann 2010); dependência parcial (curva de resposta);
% SHAP (contribuição por ponto); IndVal.g (ponte com ecologia). Lembrar a limitação de
% importância com espécies correlacionadas (Strobl 2007).
The contribution of each species was measured by out-of-fold permutation importance: within each
test fold, the records of one species were shuffled among the held-out points and the resulting drop
in pooled balanced accuracy was recorded (\res{nrep-importance} repetitions). Its significance was
assessed by recomputing the importances after permuting the landscape labels
\citep[\res{nperm-importance} permutations;][]{altmann2010}, which corrects the known biases of
impurity-based importance \citep{strobl2007}. The direction of each species' effect was described
by partial dependence \citep{friedman2001}, the average predicted probability of each landscape when
the species' frequency is set to a given value in every point, and by SHAP values, which split each
individual prediction into additive species contributions \citep{lundberg2017,lundberg2020}. Because
partial dependence of an unweighted forest can follow the larger class, we report it for both the
balanced and the unweighted forests. Finally, the group-equalised indicator value IndVal.g
\citep{dufrene1997,decaceres2009} provided the conventional community-ecology view of the same
question (Appendix~\ref{app:species}).

\subsection{Robustness (O4)}\label{sec:mrobust}
% Conteúdo: cada cenário muda UMA decisão; listar e justificar. Explicar em especial:
% blocos espaciais (Roberts 2017), n igual (11 por paisagem), remoção de A. bequaerti (o sinal
% de convergência depende de uma espécie?), e as checagens de campanha: PERMANOVA entre
% campanhas dentro da várzea; transferência entre campanhas; probabilidades por campanha
% no Pantanal.
Each robustness scenario changed a single analytical decision and re-estimated balanced accuracy,
Pantanal recall, the Pantanal-to-Cerrado error rate and the two pairwise AUCs: (i) spatially blocked
cross-validation, in which the points of each landscape were grouped into three spatial clusters and
whole clusters were held out \citep{roberts2017}; (ii) presence--absence instead of frequencies;
(iii) removal of species recorded at a single point; (iv) \res{sens-nsub} random subsamples with the
same number of points per landscape; (v) removal of \taxon{Agelaia bequaerti}, the dominant species
shared by the Pantanal and the Cerrado; and (vi--viii) alternative numbers of candidate species per
split and minimum leaf size. Because the landscapes were sampled in different campaigns, we also
quantified compositional change among campaigns within the floodplain (PERMANOVA), tested whether
floodplain points of a held-out campaign were still recognised by a model trained on the other
campaigns, and compared the predicted probabilities of Pantanal points from its two campaigns.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% OBJETIVO: interpretar, NÃO repetir números. Cinco parágrafos:

%     Soininen 2007); campanhas (Cerrado set/2025, Pantanal majoritariamente nov/2025, várzea
%     2024); n pequeno; só armadilhas atrativas (viés para espécies atraídas por iscas).
%  P5 Implicações e próximos passos: desenho com réplicas de áreas preservadas e antropizadas

\paragraph{Lost identity of the anthropised wetland.}
Social-wasp assemblages carried a clear signature of their landscape, but the signatures were not
equally distinct. The preserved floodplain was unmistakable and shared almost no species with the
other two areas (H1), whereas the anthropised Pantanal wetland could barely be told apart from the
dry Cerrado: a classifier trained on these two areas ranked a Pantanal point above a Cerrado point
only slightly more often than a coin would. Contrary to the expectation that two flood-pulse wetlands
should resemble each other, the Pantanal and Cerrado assemblages behaved almost as a single
assemblage, as predicted by H2.

\paragraph{Biological mechanisms.}
% BIÓLOGOS: revisar e completar. Os mecanismos abaixo vêm do rascunho e da literatura, mas NÃO foram
% medidos aqui (não há variáveis de vegetação nem de microclima): escrever como hipóteses.
The species that made the floodplain recognisable were swarm-founding Epiponini recorded only there,
chiefly \taxon{Agelaia pallipes} and \taxon{Polybia jurinei}. Swarm-founding species build large,
long-lived nests that depend on structurally complex vegetation, permanent water and abundant prey
\citep{richards1978,jeanne1991}, conditions that riparian forests of preserved floodplains provide
\citep{decastro2021,leite2026}. The convergence between Pantanal and Cerrado, in contrast, rested on
species recorded in both areas, above all \taxon{Agelaia bequaerti}, which occurred at most points of
both and was often the only species recorded. A plausible explanation is that the simplification of
the vegetation around Baía Negra, with more insolation and desiccation, filters out
moisture-dependent specialists and leaves an assemblage of tolerant species similar to that of a
seasonally dry savanna \citep{jacques2023,francisco2023,almeida2014}; the only species that still
singled out the Pantanal, \taxon{Polybia ruficeps}, was recorded at few points. Because no habitat
variables were measured, these mechanisms remain hypotheses. \TODO{biologists: nesting habits of
\taxon{A.~bequaerti} and \taxon{P.~ruficeps}; land use at Baía Negra.}

\paragraph{What validated classifiers add.}
The same data analysed with PERMANOVA show only that Pantanal and Cerrado do not differ
significantly, which is absence of evidence. The pairwise AUC turns this into an effect size with
an interval, on a scale with a direct reading (the probability of telling two samples apart), and
the class probabilities give its direction: Pantanal points were pulled towards the Cerrado, not
towards the other wetland. A trained classifier can also score new samples, which makes ``how much
does this site still look like a preserved floodplain?'' an operational monitoring question. The
validation steps were not a formality. With unequal classes, the unweighted forest of the first
analysis made the overlap look one-sided, as if the Cerrado had absorbed the Pantanal
(\res{rf-default-pan-to-cer} of the Pantanal points predicted as Cerrado, \res{rf-default-cer-to-pan}
in the opposite direction); with balanced classes the confusion became mutual, and the apparent
affinity of \taxon{A.~bequaerti} switched between Cerrado and Pantanal depending on class weighting.
The overlap itself is robust, but its asymmetry, emphasised in the first draft, was an artefact of
sample sizes; balanced models, a null reference and permutation tests are what allow the two to be
told apart. Likewise, a negative or null importance of a widespread species does not mean that it
is ecologically irrelevant, only that it does not help to separate the landscapes
\citep{strobl2007}.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations and limitations.}
% Este parágrafo já está redigido porque é o que mais protege o artigo na revisão.
Three features of the design limit causal inference. First, each landscape is represented by a single
protected area, so the classifier distinguishes areas rather than landscape types, and the
comparison is a case study rather than a replicated test \citep{hurlbert1984}. Second, the Pantanal
and Cerrado sites are much closer to each other (\res{dist-pan-cer}~km) than either is to the
floodplain (\res{dist-pan-fpl} and \res{dist-cer-fpl}~km) and both drain to the Paraguay River, whereas
the floodplain belongs to the Paraná basin; distance decay of similarity \citep{soininen2007} alone
could therefore produce a Pantanal--Cerrado resemblance. Third, the areas were sampled in different
campaigns: the Cerrado at the end of the 2025 dry season, most Pantanal points two months later, and
the floodplain in 2024. The within-floodplain checks suggest that campaign effects are small
relative to landscape effects, but they cannot be evaluated for the Pantanal--Cerrado pair. The
convergence we measured is therefore compatible with anthropogenic homogenisation, but a replicated
design is needed to attribute it to disturbance. Finally, only attractive traps were analysed, which
favours species attracted to fruit baits, and the sample is small, which our intervals reflect.

\paragraph{Implications.}
If confirmed by a replicated design, our results mean that protecting wetland status on paper is not
enough to keep a wetland's fauna: the anthropised Pantanal area, although protected, has a wasp
assemblage that no longer resembles that of a preserved wetland. The approach is directly transferable
to monitoring. A classifier trained on reference (preserved) sites can score any new survey, and its
predicted probability of ``preserved wetland'' is an interpretable index of lost identity. Testing
the anthropogenic cause requires several preserved and disturbed wetlands in each basin, sampled in
the same season, evaluated by leaving whole sites out of the training data, and habitat variables to
link the loss of specialists to vegetation structure. \TODO{biologists: management recommendation for
riparian vegetation at Baía Negra.}

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
% 3-4 frases. Repetir a mensagem central e a implicação. Sem números novos.
Social-wasp assemblages identify their landscape of origin far above chance. The preserved floodplain
keeps an unmistakable identity, whereas the assemblage of an anthropised Pantanal wetland has become
almost indistinguishable from that of a dry savanna, a convergence carried by a few widespread
species. With one area per landscape this is a case study compatible with biotic homogenisation, not
a demonstration of its cause. Validated, imbalance-aware classifiers and a direct pairwise test turn
``the model confused two habitats'' into a quantitative measure of lost ecological identity that can
be used to monitor wetlands.

\section{Notation, dissimilarity and community tests}\label{app:community}
Let $x_{ij}$ be the number of independent records of species $j$ at point $i$, $b_{ij}=\mathbb{1}[x_{ij}>0]$
and $A_i=\{j: b_{ij}=1\}$ the species list of point $i$; $y_i$ is its landscape, $n_k$ the number of
points of landscape $k$ and $K=3$.

\paragraph{Jaccard dissimilarity.} $d_{ii'} = 1 - |A_i\cap A_{i'}|\,/\,|A_i\cup A_{i'}|$, the
proportion of the species of two points that is not shared.

\paragraph{PCoA.} With $\mathbf{D}^{(2)}=[d_{ii'}^2]$ and the centring matrix
$\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{I}-n^{-1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$, the Gower matrix
$\mathbf{G}=-\tfrac12\mathbf{J}\mathbf{D}^{(2)}\mathbf{J}$ is diagonalised,
$\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{V}\bm{\Lambda}\mathbf{V}^\top$, and the coordinates on axis $m$ are
$\mathbf{v}_m\sqrt{\lambda_m}$ for $\lambda_m>0$ \citep{gower1966}. Axis $m$ explains
$\lambda_m/\sum_{\lambda>0}\lambda$ of the inertia.

\paragraph{PERMANOVA.} With total and within-group sums of squares
$SS_T = n^{-1}\sum_{i<i'} d_{ii'}^2$ and $SS_W=\sum_k n_k^{-1}\sum_{i<i',\,y_i=y_{i'}=k} d_{ii'}^2$,
\[
F=\frac{(SS_T-SS_W)/(K-1)}{SS_W/(n-K)},\qquad R^2 = 1-\frac{SS_W}{SS_T},
\]
and $p=(1+\#\{F^{(\pi)}\ge F\})/(1+N_\pi)$ over $N_\pi$ random permutations $\pi$ of the labels
\citep{anderson2001}. Pairwise tests use the points of two landscapes; $p$-values are Holm-adjusted.

\paragraph{PERMDISP.} Each point's distance $z_i$ to the centroid of its landscape in the full PCoA
space (negative eigenvalues handled as in \citealp{anderson2006}) enters a one-way ANOVA; residuals
$z_i-\bar z_{y_i}$ are permuted to obtain the null distribution of $F$.

\paragraph{IndVal.g.}\label{app:species} For species $j$ and landscape $k$, specificity
$A_{kj}=\bar x_{kj}/\sum_{l}\bar x_{lj}$ (with group means $\bar x_{kj}$, i.e. equalised group sizes)
and fidelity $B_{kj}=n_k^{-1}\sum_{i:y_i=k}b_{ij}$ combine into
$\mathrm{IndVal.g}_{kj}=\sqrt{A_{kj}B_{kj}}$; the species is assigned to the landscape with the
largest value, tested by label permutation \citep{dufrene1997,decaceres2009}.

\section{Multinomial logistic regression}\label{app:logistic}
\[
P(y=k\mid\mathbf{x})=\frac{\exp(\beta_{0k}+\bm\beta_k^\top\tilde{\mathbf{x}})}
{\sum_{l=1}^{K}\exp(\beta_{0l}+\bm\beta_l^\top\tilde{\mathbf{x}})},
\]
where $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ are frequencies standardised with the training-fold mean and standard
deviation. Parameters minimise
$-\sum_i w_{y_i}\log P(y_i\mid\mathbf{x}_i)+\tfrac{1}{2C}\sum_k\|\bm\beta_k\|_2^2$ with $C=1$ and class
weights $w_k=n/(K n_k)$. A positive $\beta_{kj}$ means that recording species $j$ raises the odds of
landscape $k$. \emph{Why:} the simplest model that can use all species jointly; if it performed as
well as the forests, non-linear interactions would be unnecessary.

\section{$k$-nearest neighbours with Jaccard distance}\label{app:knn}
A point is assigned to the most frequent landscape among its $k=5$ nearest training points in Jaccard
dissimilarity, and $\hat P(y=k\mid\mathbf{x})$ is the fraction of those neighbours from landscape $k$.
\emph{Why:} it is the predictive version of the similarity reasoning of community ecology (``this
sample looks like those''), so its errors show what composition alone implies, without any model.

\section{Random forests and balanced random forests}\label{app:rf}
A classification tree recursively splits the points; at a node with class proportions $p_k$, the
split on species $j$ at threshold $t$ that maximises the decrease in Gini impurity
$G=1-\sum_k p_k^2$ is chosen. A random forest averages $B$ trees,
\[
\hat P(y=k\mid\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\hat P_b(y=k\mid\mathbf{x}),
\]
each grown on a bootstrap sample of the points and choosing each split among $m_{\mathrm{try}}=
\lfloor\sqrt{p}\rfloor$ randomly drawn species \citep{breiman2001}. Averaging many decorrelated trees
reduces variance while keeping the ability of trees to represent interactions (``\taxon{A.~pallipes}
present \emph{and} \taxon{P.~ruficeps} absent'') and to ignore irrelevant zeros. In the
\emph{class-weighted} forest, each point of class $k$ has weight $n/(Kn_k)$ within every bootstrap
sample. In the \emph{balanced} random forest \citep{chen2004}, each tree is grown on a sample drawn
with replacement containing the same number of points (the size of the smallest class) from every
class. \emph{Why:} the forest is the standard flexible model for community data
\citep{cutler2007}; balancing prevents the larger classes from attracting the errors of the smaller
one, which is essential when the confusion of the smallest class (Pantanal) is the object of the
hypothesis.

\section{Gradient boosting}\label{app:boosting}
Boosting builds an additive model $F_M(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\nu f_m(\mathbf{x})$ of shallow trees
$f_m$ (depth 2), each fitted to the negative gradient of the multinomial log-loss of the current model,
with learning rate $\nu=0.05$, $M=200$ iterations, at least four points per leaf and class weights
\citep{friedman2001}. \emph{Why:} a different way of combining trees (sequential error correction
rather than averaging); agreement with the forests shows that the conclusions do not depend on one
algorithm.

\section{Validation, metrics and permutation tests}\label{app:validation}
For repetition $r=1,\dots,R$ the points are split into five stratified folds; every point $i$ receives
a prediction $\hat y_i^{(r)}$ and probabilities $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i^{(r)}$ from the model trained
without its fold. With recall $\mathrm{Rec}_k=\#\{i:y_i=k,\hat y_i=k\}/n_k$,
\[
\mathrm{BA}=\frac1K\sum_{k}\mathrm{Rec}_k,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e},\qquad
\mathrm{LL}=-\frac1n\sum_i\log\hat p_{i,y_i},
\]
where $p_o$ is the observed agreement and $p_e$ the agreement expected from the marginal frequencies
\citep{cohen1960,brodersen2010}. Each metric is computed per repetition, and its mean and 2.5--97.5
percentiles are reported. The permutation test recomputes the cross-validated BA after permuting the
labels, $p=(1+\#\{\mathrm{BA}^{(\pi)}\ge\mathrm{BA}\})/(1+N_\pi)$, which tests whether the model has
learned any association between composition and landscape \citep{ojala2010}.

\section{Pairwise AUC and paired bootstrap}\label{app:auc}
For landscapes $a$ (positive) and $b$, let $s_i$ be the out-of-fold probability of $a$ for point $i$,
averaged over repetitions. The AUC is the Mann--Whitney statistic
\[
\AUC_{a\times b}=\frac{1}{n_a n_b}\sum_{i:y_i=a}\sum_{i':y_{i'}=b}
\left(\mathbb{1}[s_i>s_{i'}]+\tfrac12\mathbb{1}[s_i=s_{i'}]\right).
\]
For $\Delta=\AUC_{\Pan\times\Fpl}-\AUC_{\Pan\times\Cer}$, each bootstrap resample draws Pantanal,
floodplain and Cerrado points with replacement within landscapes, using the same Pantanal draw in both
AUCs; the 2.5--97.5 percentiles of $\Delta^{*}$ give the interval and $P(\Delta^{*}\le0)$ the one-sided
bootstrap $p$-value \citep{efron1993}.

\section{Permutation importance, partial dependence and SHAP}\label{app:importance}
\paragraph{Out-of-fold permutation importance.} For species $j$, within each test fold the column $j$
is permuted among the held-out points and the fold is predicted again; with $\mathrm{BA}$ the pooled
out-of-fold balanced accuracy,
$I_j = \mathrm{BA}-\frac1Q\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\mathrm{BA}^{(\pi_j,q)}$, averaged over repetitions. Computing
it on held-out points measures how much the model's \emph{generalisation} depends on $j$. The
$p$-value compares $I_j$ with importances recomputed after permuting the labels \citep{altmann2010}.

\paragraph{Partial dependence.} $\mathrm{PD}_{jk}(v)=\frac1n\sum_i\hat P(y=k\mid x_{ij}=v,\mathbf{x}_{i,-j})$,
the mean predicted probability of landscape $k$ when species $j$ is set to $v$ records at every point
\citep{friedman2001}. It describes a species' response curve as seen by the model.

\paragraph{SHAP.} For a prediction $f(\mathbf{x})$, the Shapley value of species $j$ is
\[
\phi_j=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{j\}}\frac{|S|!\,(p-|S|-1)!}{p!}
\left[f_{S\cup\{j\}}(\mathbf{x})-f_S(\mathbf{x})\right],
\]
the average change in prediction when $j$ joins a coalition $S$ of species; the $\phi_j$ add up to
the prediction minus the mean prediction. TreeSHAP computes them exactly for tree ensembles
\citep{lundberg2017,lundberg2020}. Importance measures share credit among co-occurring species
\citep{strobl2007}, so they are interpreted together with occurrence patterns (Fig.~\ref{fig:importance}b).

\section{Spatially blocked cross-validation}\label{app:spatial}
Within each landscape, point coordinates (converted to km) are grouped into three clusters by
$k$-means; fold $b$ holds out cluster $b$ of every landscape, with the pairing of clusters to folds
randomised in each repetition. Test points therefore have no spatial neighbours of their own cluster in
the training data, which removes the optimism caused by spatial autocorrelation within areas
\citep{roberts2017,ploton2020}. It cannot address differences between areas, because each landscape
is a single area.
